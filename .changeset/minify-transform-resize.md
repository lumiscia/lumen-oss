`transform` and `resize` filter a source they show smaller than its size, so a 4K image or video shrunk to a thumbnail, or a zoom-out animation, no longer aliases and shimmers. Each output pixel averages the premultiplied linear light of the source texels it covers, weighted by area, along each of the source's axes, so shrinking one axis leaves the other as sharp as it was. The filter is bilinear at a scale of 1 and widens smoothly from there, and it takes the box on the source itself down to a quarter of its size, and past that on its mip levels, blending the two around the one the scale asks for, so an animated zoom changes smoothly. A source shown between a quarter and its full size builds no mip chain. The mip chain is built once for a source that stays the same from frame to frame and each frame for one that changes, and a source that several nodes shrink shares one. Each tap of a motion-blurred `transform` is filtered for its own scale. `sampling: "linear"` does this; `nearest` takes the nearest texel as before. Magnification and scales of 1 or more are unchanged pixel for pixel, rotation at a scale of 1 included. The mip chains the engine keeps, which 3D scenes also use, have a byte budget, `CacheBudgets::mip_chains` (256 MiB, as before), and `CacheStats::mip_chains` reports what they hold. There is no new param.
